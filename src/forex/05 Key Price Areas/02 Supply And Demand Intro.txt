    Supply and demand is the "why" behind support and resistance. S/R tells you where price reacts — supply/demand tells you why it reacts there.

    Demand zone = area where buyers were so aggressive
              that price left FAST and didn't look back

    Supply zone  = area where sellers were so aggressive
                that price left FAST and didn't look back
    
    The key word is fast. A slow grind away from a level = weak zone. A sharp impulsive candle away = strong zone. That speed tells you someone with serious size entered there.

    Demand zone that breaks = becomes supply zone
    Supply zone that breaks = becomes demand zone

    Same logic as S/R role reversal, just more precise origin


Visually — the 4 zone patterns
    There are 4 ways a zone is created. Two for demand, two for supply:
    DEMAND (buying zones):
    1. Rally-Base-Rally    (RBR)  ← continuation, trend with you
    2. Drop-Base-Rally     (DBR)  ← reversal, counter trend

    SUPPLY (selling zones):
    1. Drop-Base-Drop      (DBD)  ← continuation, trend with you
    2. Rally-Base-Drop     (RBD)  ← reversal, counter trend

    What "Base" means:
        The base is the consolidation/pause candles sitting between the two big moves. It's usually 1–5 small candles where price rested before the explosion. That base = the zone you mark and wait for price to return to.

    RBR example (demand, continuation):
    ↑ strong move up
    → 2-3 small sideways candles  ← MARK THIS as demand zone
    ↑ another strong move up
    → price returns to that base = buy opportunity

    DBR example (demand, reversal):
    ↓ strong move down
    → 1-3 small candles           ← MARK THIS as demand zone
    ↑ sudden strong move up
    → price returns to that base = buy opportunity

how to draw supply and demand S&D zones
    method1:
        minimum three momentum candle in a row either direction
        draw rectangle from the hight and low of previous candle of the momentum candle

        Step 1: Find the sharp impulsive move (the "explosion candle")
        Step 2: Look left — find where that move started
        Step 3: Mark the BASE candles just before the explosion
        Step 4: Zone top = high of the base candles
                Zone bottom = low of the base candles
        Step 5: Wait for price to return to that zone
        Step 6: Look for confirmation candle at the zone edge
        Step 7: Enter with SL beyond the zone, TP at next zone

    method2:
        Look for Consolidation
        draw a rectangle where price keep moving sideways
    
    method3
        look fro wicks
        draw a reactangle on wick where price is mostly rejected [up or down]

high quality zones vs low quality zones
    STRONG zone has:
    ✓ Sharp impulsive move away (big candle, fast move)
    ✓ Small tight base (1-3 candles, not a wide range)
    ✓ Fresh — not yet revisited (first touch is strongest)
    ✓ Visible on higher timeframe (H4 or Daily)
    ✓ Left quickly — price didn't hang around

    WEAK zone has:
    ✗ Slow grinding move away
    ✗ Wide messy base (choppy candles)
    ✗ Already tested multiple times (liquidity consumed)
    ✗ Only visible on low timeframe (M5, M15)


    First touch rule — this is critical and most beginners miss it:
        First time price returns to a zone = highest probability
        Second touch = still valid but weaker
        Third touch = zone is likely broken or exhausted

        Why? Each time price revisits a zone, the orders sitting
        there get filled. Eventually they run out. By the 3rd touch
        the zone is often empty — price breaks through.
        This is the opposite of S/R where more touches = stronger. In supply/demand, more touches = weaker.

The full entry checklist with supply/demand:
    DEMAND ZONE ENTRY (long):
    1. Identify trend on H4/Daily
    2. Find fresh demand zone (RBR or DBR pattern)
    3. Zone aligns with HTF context
    4. Price returns to zone for first or second time
    5. Confirmation candle at zone (hammer, engulfing)
    6. SL below the zone bottom
    7. TP at nearest supply zone above

    SUPPLY ZONE ENTRY (short):
    1. Identify trend on H4/Daily
    2. Find fresh supply zone (DBD or RBD pattern)
    3. Zone aligns with HTF context
    4. Price returns to zone for first or second time
    5. Confirmation candle at zone (shooting star, engulfing)
    6. SL above the zone top
    7. TP at nearest demand zone below




Supply vs Resistance / Demand vs Support — the difference
    Support/Resistance:
    Drawn as lines or zones at obvious price levels
    Based on where price bounced historically
    Everyone sees them = easily manipulated (stop hunts)

    Supply/Demand zones:
    Based on the ORIGIN of an impulsive move
    More specific — you're marking exactly where
    the institutional order was placed
    Less obvious = harder to fake out
